Tribe AI builds AI products and agents for some of the world’s largest companies in regulated industries like financial services, healthcare, legal and insurance. This year, Tribe expects to grow its enterprise business sixfold and its team fivefold, with Claude helping the company scale while preserving the speed of a much smaller team. New engineers who once took three to four months to onboard now ship code within five days. Customers get a production agent running in about a month instead of six. Everything Tribe proves on itself first, then brings to customers, combining those proven patterns with deep AI expertise.

We spoke with co-founder and CEO Jaclyn Rice Nelson and CTO Pooja Brown about being both an Anthropic partner and a Claude customer, managing risk for regulated customers, and what enterprise leaders can take from Tribe’s experience.

### What does Tribe do?

**Jaclyn Rice Nelson:** We build AI products for the largest companies in the world. When I started Tribe, it was because I saw that AI would be the future of every business. I also saw how hard it could be to adopt these technologies, because they were so new and so dynamic. So I set out to build Tribe to fill the gap between the AI technology coming out of companies like Anthropic and enterprises’ ability to not just use it, but actually get value out of it. The question I ask any of our customers is this: if you were to rebuild your company today with all of the capabilities of AI, would you operate the way you operate today? The answer is always no. Customers want us to deliver value with Claude, but they also want to know how to do this themselves.

**Pooja Brown:** We’re not here to sell AI. We’re here to sell outcomes. How can I do things faster? How can I do things better? How can I differentiate myself with my customer? How can I reduce cost? A lot of companies take little pieces and automate them with Claude. What we want to do is change your entire development lifecycle, from the minute you finish a meeting to shipping the outcome of that meeting to production. Gone are the debates of monolith versus microservices. Those have become Claude’s problem. What’s left is human judgment, human experience, and understanding the real problem you’re trying to solve.

### Tribe is both an Anthropic partner and a Claude customer. What does that mean in practice?

**Jaclyn Rice Nelson:** We’ve been using Claude since the beginning. Anthropic was one of our early customers. Tribe has gone from working for Anthropic, to working with Anthropic, to working with Anthropic’s customers.

Working together on customer engagements feels so natural because we have that shared DNA: the focus on speed and outcomes, underpinned by a belief in what the technology can do. We try to operate a bit ahead of the market. That helps us find the gaps when we implement in large enterprises. Then it’s on Tribe to build solutions that fill those gaps.

### How fast is Tribe growing, and what slowed you down?

**Pooja Brown:** As we scaled, the challenge became getting the right context to the right person without relying on more meetings, handoffs, or what happened to be in someone’s head. The engineers we hire are extremely skilled, but the time it took to get them productive would be months. We would measure time of joining to time to first PR, and you’d look at three to four months. Think of how many meetings, how many conversations, how much tapping on somebody’s shoulder you’d have to do just to figure out whether the change you’re about to make is going to produce the value you need. Now they are shipping in days, because frontier models like Claude have given us the tools and access to give engineers the right context they need to be productive in their jobs.

We built a shared knowledge system, which is essentially a Git-based brain. As you make decisions as an engineer, we record them. If you join the project, you can just plug yourself into my brain. So now you plug engineers into a team, into an account. Day three, they know exactly what they need to be doing. In less than five days, they’re producing code changes. Every time we walk into a new account, we’re not starting from zero. We’re already starting from 60 because we bring the patterns, tooling and lessons from everything we’ve already built.

### How did Claude change the way Tribe worked with a Fortune 100 Retailer?

**Jaclyn Rice Nelson:** A global consumer brand came to Anthropic with a high priority, customer-facing flagship use case backed by senior leadership. We met with the Anthropic sales team, and walked into a two-day on-site, using the first day to go deep into requirements. Overnight, we used Claude to build a fully functioning prototype that was exactly what the customer had imagined in their own words, but had never seen brought to life. It was no longer theoretical. Within two months of that first conversation, we kicked off the project.

### How did Tribe use AI to capture one of America’s largest private companies’ way of thinking?

**Jaclyn Rice Nelson:** In many enterprises, the knowledge, the judgment, is actually not documented. So it’s not a problem of, “Hey Claude, go connect to my Google Drive.” It’s, “Hey Claude, we need to capture what’s in people’s heads.”

One of the largest privately held companies in America has a deeply codified set of operating principles that govern how they make decisions. They have a full team of coaches who push employees to think bigger and take calculated swings. We codified that judgment, which used to be coached individually, in a public Socratic coaching app. It was a way of democratizing access to this way of thinking. Now we’re using Claude to bring that judgment into where work gets done every day, for every employee.

### How has Claude changed the way you lead?

**Jaclyn Rice Nelson:** CEOs can create a lot of chaos for their teams. I’m constantly tempted to send Slack messages asking for things. So we built a go-to-market analyst with Claude. I now just talk to my little agent. I can run into my next investor meeting with all the data at my fingertips. It’s about preserving team focus.
