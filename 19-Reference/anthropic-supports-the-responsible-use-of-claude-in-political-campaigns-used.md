Anthropic supports the responsible use of Claude in political campaigns. Used well, Claude can
help campaigns work faster, communicate more clearly, and reach voters more effectively, all of
which contribute to a better-informed electorate. This guide outlines both the many ways
campaigns can use Claude and the uses that are prohibited under Anthropic’s Usage Policy.*


Ways campaigns can use Claude
Writing and content development
   • Drafting and editing emails, press statements, and social media posts from
     official campaign accounts to help engage and communicate with voters and
     constituents.
   • Designing graphics, fliers, and websites, from first concept to final copy
   • Producing longform articles and blog posts
   • Preparing speeches, stump remarks, and debate talking points

Research and analysis
   • Reviewing polling data for trends
   • Summarizing and analyzing policy documents, bills, and regulatory filings
   • Drawing themes from voter surveys and town hall comments
   • Analyzing turnout and precinct data to prioritize time and resources

Voter correspondence
   • Triaging constituent correspondence and routing it to the right staff
   • Building an FAQ or knowledge base for volunteers and voters

Prohibited uses
The following uses of Claude are prohibited under our policy because they undermine election
integrity and the public trust that legitimate campaigns depend on. Anthropic enforces these
limits and accounts engaged in the activity outlined below may face enforcement action, up to
and including a ban.

Impersonation and fake personas
   • Creating or distributing fake online personas to amplify content, including to
     criticize and undermine an opponent
   • Fabricating online personas to resemble real voters and presenting them as
     organic voices

Example: using Claude to write profiles and posts for social media accounts that pose as real
people discussing politics.

Manufactured support and astroturfing
   • Building bot networks or “sockpuppet” accounts
   • Artificially amplifying engagement, such as likes, shares, and comments

Voter suppression
   • Disseminating false or misleading information about when, where, or how to
     vote
   • Targeting eligible voters or specific demographics with content intended to
     discourage them from participating

Example: using Claude to create social media posts telling a neighborhood its polling place has
moved or closed when it has not.

Deepfakes and disinformation
   • Generating undisclosed synthetic media that impersonates a candidate
     (including on behalf of one’s own campaign), voter, or entity associated with an
     election
   • Creating content designed to mislead the public about an election

Example: using Claude to script a fake video or audio clip of an opponent saying something they
never said.

* All users are responsible for compliance with applicable laws and regulations. In particular,
election laws may require public communications to contain certain disclaimers depending on
the content, distribution, and timing of the communication. Users should ensure that all
Claude-generated or assisted content contains the applicable disclaimers for their jurisdiction.
