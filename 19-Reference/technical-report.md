Preclinical Characterization of a Bispecific Bridging
Antibody for the Treatment of LAMA2-Related Congenital
Muscular Dystrophy
Cody Tranbarger1,* and Mithril Medicine†

Abstract
LAMA2-related congenital muscular dystrophy (LAMA2-CMD) is a devastating pediatric disease caused by the loss of
laminin-α2 for which there is no disease-modifying treatment. Laminin-α2 is the α chain of laminin-211, the heterotrimer
that assembles the basement membrane around each muscle fiber and anchors it to the sarcolemma. Muscle lacking
laminin-α2 overexpresses a related laminin, laminin-α4, but the protein fails to self-polymerize and binds α-dystroglycan
only weakly, and thus fails to compensate for the missing laminin-α2. We hypothesized that a bispecific antibody bridging
laminin-α4 to β-dystroglycan (β-DG), the transmembrane subunit of dystroglycan, would restore basement membrane
anchorage and sarcolemma stability. We engineered such bispecific bridging antibodies in several formats and studied
two in depth, one bivalent and one tetravalent. Both bound their targets with nanomolar affinity and bridged dystroglycan-
overexpressing cells to a recombinant fragment of laminin-α4. In a pilot study in laminin-α2-deficient dyW mice, the
bivalent antibody was recovered from muscle days after last dose, at concentrations exceeding those required for bridging
in vitro. Meanwhile, mice treated with the tetravalent construct developed about twice the peak force and grip strength
of vehicle-treated mice. These data identify antibody design as a major determinant of bridging efficiency, suggest
that reaching translationally relevant antibody exposures in muscle is feasible, and provide proof of principle for the
therapeutic approach, supporting the continued progression of Anthropic’s bispecific bridging antibody programs in
LAMA2-CMD and α-dystroglycanopathy.
